Welcome to Raha

Raha (رها) means “set free.” Here, tabs don’t spend your memory — you do, on purpose.

Go somewhere

Press Ctrl+L (or click the bar up top), type a search or an address, hit Enter. Search is DuckDuckGo by default.

The live bar is the truth

The strip at the bottom shows every tab that is actually running with its real memory and CPU, measured from the OS. If it's not there, it's costing you nothing.

Hover a chip and hit the moon to put it to sleep yourself.

Tabs sleep — that's the point

You have a cap (6 live tabs to start; change it in Settings). Open a seventh and the one you touched longest ago goes to sleep: its process is destroyed, the memory returns to your Mac or PC, and its card stays right where it was. Click any sleeping card to wake it.

Restarting Raha brings your whole workspace back asleep — instant start, near-zero memory, nothing lost. Try the “Try these” folder in the sidebar: those tabs are asleep right now, costing 0 MB.

Protect what matters

Pin a tab (Ctrl+Shift+K or the pin icon) and Raha will never auto-sleep it. Music keeps playing — tabs making sound are protected by default. Give a hungry tab its own limit with the gauge icon: cross it, and it sleeps, pinned or not.

Power move: Settings → Domain rules. *.music.youtube.com → keep alive. *.slack.com → 800 MB, max.

Organize spatially

Folders nest as deep as you like. Drag tabs between them in the sidebar. Click a folder to see its tabs as thumbnail cards. Right-click anything for more — including “sleep all tabs inside.” Ctrl+E brings you back to the grid.

Private by architecture

No telemetry, ever. Raha itself contacts one server: GitHub Releases, to check for security updates so you get Chromium's fixes — turn it off in Settings. (Your tab list also loads each site's favicon from that site.) Ads and trackers are blocked with the bundled EasyList + EasyPrivacy lists — watch the shield counter in the address bar, and click it to allow a site that misbehaves. Sites asking for your location, camera, or notifications get told no, visibly. This page loaded nothing from the internet.
